# Northstar Telecom fictional dossier

Northstar Telecom, Maya Chen, and every account detail in this file are fictional. Northstar is not an ElevenLabs customer. The learner is always from an external ElevenLabs SI partner organization, never an ElevenLabs employee.

## Service organization

Northstar is a regional telecom provider with 420 contact-center agents supporting residential customers. Repeat contacts concern outage updates, billing explanations, and order status. Call spikes create customer waiting while agents spend time repeating routine updates. No financial baseline, quantified customer impact, or ROI is known.

## Current human workflow

Agents work across CRM, ticketing, and telephony systems. Vendors, versions, APIs, data contracts, and integration details are unknown. An agent checks service and customer context, responds to the customer, and documents the contact. Complex, sensitive, and ambiguous requests stay with people. Handoff context, failure paths, and operational ownership need discovery.

## Maya's priorities

Maya Chen is VP of Customer Experience. She wants faster routine answers without making support feel less human. She is accountable for customer experience, service quality, and the operational impact of change. She cares about customer adoption, human escalation, integration effort, testing, monitoring, reliability, and enterprise deployment. She is interested in exploration, not an approved purchase.

## Likely stakeholders

- Support operations: workflow feasibility, agent adoption, baseline measurement, and day-to-day service ownership.
- IT: systems, data access, integration constraints, architecture, and operational support.
- Security: privacy, data access, residency, retention, and compliance review.
- Procurement: commercial process, contracts, and paper process.
- Maya: executive sponsor for continued discovery only. Economic-buyer authority is unknown.

## Explicit unknowns

Budget, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, success baseline, implementation timing, competition, integration architecture, data access, telephony requirements, security obligations, deployment constraints, and support model are all unknown. The partner must ask rather than fill these gaps with assumptions.

## Safe seller path

Discover repeat-contact drivers, the human workflow, impact, desired outcomes, stakeholders, and decision process. Frame voice and chat agents as a hypothesis for faster routine answers and more human time for complex work. Separate official product capabilities and named customer stories from Northstar's unproven business case.

The SI can propose paid discovery, validation, implementation, and ongoing optimization as possible services if the customer case justifies them. This is a partner revenue hypothesis, not a promised higher billable rate, guaranteed deployment, or invented customer ROI. Close on a bounded next step with an owner and timing.

## Safe solutions-consultant path

Trace one request through customer intent, agent lookup, response, documentation, and human escalation. Discover systems, data, permissions, integration constraints, handoff context, testing, monitoring, failure paths, reliability, deployment requirements, and operational ownership. Use official documentation to frame questions, not to assert Northstar compatibility.

Bring in an FDE or another specialist for detailed telephony or integration architecture, security and compliance commitments, unsupported ROI or proof, production-readiness judgments, and implementation commitments. Close on technical discovery or validation with explicit inputs and unknowns.
